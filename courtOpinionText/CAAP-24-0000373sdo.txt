Brown argues: (1) the circuit court lacked jurisdiction 
to resentence him because "the proceedings involving Brown's deferral have exceeded the 'maximum sentence allowable' mandated under" Hawaii Revised Statutes (HRS) § 853-1;
6 and (2) the delay 
in resentencing deprived him of due process in violation of article I, section 5 of the Hawai# i Constitution and the 
Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution. 
The State argues Brown's appeal is moot because he 
completed his five-year sentence. "[A] case is moot if the reviewing court can no longer grant effective relief." Kaho#ohanohano v. State, 114 Hawai# i 302, 332, 162 P.3d 696, 726 
(2007). Brown has served his five-year sentence. We cannot grant him effective relief. 
His appeal is moot. See State v. 
Kiese, 126 Hawai# i 494, 508, 273 P.3d 1180, 1194 (2012) (appeal 
from denial of motion to stay sentence was moot because sentence was satisfied during pendency of the appeal). 
Brown counters that the collateral consequences and 
public interest exceptions to the mootness doctrine should be applied. 
The collateral consequences exception applies if a 
litigant shows there is a reasonable possibility that prejudicial

Brown argues his conviction in this case could affect 
the conditions of his incarceration and decisions by the Hawaii Paroling Authority when he applies for parole in the Boinville Murder case. In light of the offenses for which he was convicted and the sentence he received in the Boinville Murder case, the chance that his conviction and sentence in this case would have future consequences for his imprisonment and possible parole is nothing "more than mere conjecture[.]" Id.
The factors for analyzing the public interest exception 
are: (1) the public or private nature of the question presented; (2) the desirability of an authoritative determination for future guidance of public officers; and (3) the likelihood of future recurrence of the question. Id. at 6-7, 193 P.3d at 844-45. 
The issue in this appeal — setting aside a DANC plea 
after the deferral time has expired — is of a public nature. But a determination already exists. See State v. Kaufman, 92 Hawai# i 
322, 330, 991 P.2d 832, 840 (2000) (holding that moving to set aside a deferred acceptance of guilty plea tolls the deferral period until the date the court rules on the motion). And, because of Brown's parallel involvement in the Boinville Murder case, it does not appear the specific circumstances of this case are likely to recur. 
This appeal is dismissed as moot. DATED: Honolulu, Hawai# i, October 6, 2026.
