III. DISCUSSION 
Hawaii Revised Statutes ( HRS) § 580-47(a) and (d) 
(2018) authorizes the Family Court, post-divorce decree, to 
"amend or revise any order" upon a showing of "a material change 
in the physical or financial circumstances of either party," and to make "any further orders as shall appear just and equitable" upon finding that "good cause exists[.]" Modification of a spousal support order requires the family court to assess the overall relevant circumstances of the 
parties to determine whether there has been a material change in 
any of the following four relevant circumstances: (1) the payee's need; (2) the payee's ability to meet his or her need 
4
  
       NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAI ʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER 
 
 
  
 without spousal support; (3) the payor's need; and (4) the 
payor's ability to pay spousal support. Vorfeld, 8 Haw. App. at 402-03, 804 P.2d at 897. The family court must apply the following three-part analysis: (1) have any of the relevant 
circumstances materially changed?; (2) if the answer to question 
1 is yes, should there be a modification?; and (3) if the answer to question 2 is yes, what should the modification be? Weinberg v. Dickson-Weinberg, No. CAAP-15-0000362, 2016 WL 3144328, at *5 n.6 (Haw. App. May 31, 2016) (mem. op.) (quoting Vorfeld, 8 Haw. App. at 402-03, 804 P.2d at 897-98). "Any part of the payor's 
current inability to pay that was unreasonably caused by the 
payor may not be considered"; and "[a]ny part of the payee's current need" caused by the payee's violation of the "duty to exert reasonable efforts to attain self-sufficiency . . . may not be considered." Vorfeld, 8 Haw. App. at 403, 804 P.2d at 898 (citation omitted). "A material change in the relevant circumstances has occurred when a party's relevant circumstances 
that are proven to exist at the time of the modification hearing 
are materially different from the party's relevant circumstances that were proven to exist when the family court entered its spousal support order." Id. at 402, 804 P.2d at 897. The burden is on the party moving for a modification under HRS § 580-47 to prove his or her entitlement to a modification. 
Saromines v. Saromines, 3 Haw. App. 20, 28, 641 P.2d 1342, 1348 
(App. 1982).

3. COLs 9, 10 and 12 
Regarding her challenge to COLs 9, 10, and 12 that 
concluded the 2015 alimony award was "transitional," Susan argues that the 2015 divorce decree explicitly stated that 
alimony would continue "for an indefinite period of time," not a

Brendan counters that the family court in its 2015

Susan's challenge to COL 9, that the Family Court 
erroneously concluded the alimony award was "transitional," has merit. The 2015 divorce decree ordered $1800.00 of monthly alimony "continuing for an indefinite period of time thereafter." (Emphasis added.) The family court's September 17, 2015 oral ruling awarded "indefinite" alimony, subject to a 
motion raising "material changes of circumstances," as follows: 
I'm going to award alimony in the amount of
$1800.00 a month. That's for an indefinite period of
time, but that would, of course, be subject to
material changes of circumstances at which time
either party can file a motion with the Court to havethat looked at again. 
(Emphases added.) 
"Transitional" or "rehabilitative" alimony "usu[ally] 
has time limitations, such as a maximum of one or two years[,]"

 and its purpose is "to assist a divorced person in acquiring the

reflects that the indefinite $1,800.00 per month spousal support 
payments in this case were not for a transitional or 
rehabilitative purpose of allowing Susan a gradual adjustment to a reduced standard of living. 
While it appeared to award 
permanent alimony, the family court indicated that its award was 
subject to future review by a motion raising a material change 
in circumstance, which is consistent with HRS § 580-47(d). 
IV. CONCLUSION 
While we affirm certain factual findings the Family 
Court made, we vacate the Order Granting Post-Decree Relief as exceeding the scope of the Family Court's discretion, in light of the errors and incomplete analyses set forth above. On 
remand, the Family Court must complete its three-part analyses 
under Vorfeld for the four relevant circumstances; determine
