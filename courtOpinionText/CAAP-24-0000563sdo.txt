(1) First, Gramley argues the circuit court abused 
its discretion by limiting the testimony of her expert, 
Steven D. McCarus, M.D. (Dr. McCarus), to the opinions in his 
expert report (POE 1). Gramley relies on HRCP Rule 26(e)(1). 
HRCP Rule 26(e)(1) establishes a party's continuing 
duty to seasonably supplement discovery responses and 
disclosures, including the substance of expert witness 
testimony. This rule, however, does not allow parties to circumvent the deadline for expert disclosures. 
Gramley's deadline for expert disclosures in this case 
was November 19, 2023. During voir dire, Dr. McCarus 
acknowledged that none of the opinions in his report concerned 
the care of Queen's nursing staff, and that the only opinion 
rendered as to the nursing staff was in his deposition.

 Dr. McCarus was deposed on February 29, 2024, three 
months after expert disclosures were due and only three weeks before trial was scheduled to begin on March 18, 2024. As such, 
the additional opinions regarding the nursing staff were 
untimely. See Glover v. Grace Pac. Corp., 86 Hawaiʻ i 154, 163-
64, 948 P.2d 575, 584-85 (App. 1997) (holding that the trial 
court did not abuse its discretion by excluding all of 
plaintiff's documents requested by defendant but not produced

Under these circumstances, the circuit court did not 
abuse its discretion by confining Dr. McCarus's testimony to the 
contents of his report.

"relevant evidence" as "evidence having any tendency to make the  
existence of any fact that is of consequence to the 
determination of the action more probable or less probable than  
it would be without the evidence." 
 We review a trial court's 
relevance determination pursuant to HRE Rule 401 under the  
right/wrong standard. State v. Pulse, 83 Hawaiʻ i 2 29, 247, 925 
P.2d 797, 815 (1996).

physician.

 "Hearsay is not admissible except as provided by these 
rules, or by other rules prescribed by the [Hawaiʻ i Supreme 
Court], or by statute."  HRE Rule 802. 
"Hearsay" is defined as 
"a statement, other than one made by the declarant while 
testifying at the trial or hearing, offered in evidence to prove the truth of the matter asserted." HRE Rule 801. 
The proponent of hearsay bears the burden of 
establishing that the foundational requirements of the 
applicable exception are met. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. v. 
Behrendt
, 142 Hawaiʻi 37, 45-46, 414 P.3d 89, 97-98 (2018) 
(holding that the proponent failed to satisfy the foundational 
requirements for admission of business records because the 
witness was not qualified to authenticate the documents attached 
to her declaration and therefore could not establish the 
requisite foundation under the applicable exception); 
State v. 
Zukevich
, 84 Hawaiʻi 203, 206-08, 932 P.2d 340, 343-45 
(App. 1997) (holding that a homicide victim's statement, made 
moments before he was fatally shot, was admissible because the proponent satisfied the foundational requirements for admission 
under the excited utterance and present sense impression 
exceptions).

 Otherwise, the proponent must establish personal 
knowledge as a general foundation for admissibility. HRE 
Rule 602 ("A witness may not testify to a matter unless evidence  
is introduced sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge of the matter."); Nationstar Mortg. LLC  
v. Kanahele, 144 Hawaiʻ i 394, 403-04, 443 P.3d 86, 95-96 (2019) 
(holding that the loan servicer's representative lacked personal

(4) Fourth, Gramley contends that the circuit court 
improperly denied her for-cause challenge to a prospective juror (Juror 12) because Juror 12 was a current Queen's employee and 
it "did so in a prejudicial manner by making the for-cause 
challenge be done in front of the other jurors rather than a 
side bar" (POE 4). 
Under Hawaiʻ i law, "any party may challenge for cause 
any juror drawn for the trial." State v. Carvalho, 79 Hawaiʻ i 
165, 170 n.4, 880 P.2d 217, 222 n.4 (App. 1994) (quoting HRS § 635-28 (1985)). "[T]he paramount question in determining 
whether to excuse for cause a prospective juror is whether the 
defendant would be afforded a fair and impartial trial based on 
the law and evidence, with the prospective juror as a member of 
the jury." State v. Baron, 80 
Hawaiʻi 107, 114, 905 P.2d 613,

 620 (1995). The decision of whether to excuse a prospective 
juror for cause "must be left to the sound discretion of the 
trial judge who is in a better position than the appellate court 
to ascertain from the answers of the juror whether the juror is 
able to be fair and impartial." Id. 
Gramley's counsel initially "pass[ed] for cause," but 
then stated that, "being a Queen's employee, it's probably 
better that we find another case that [Juror 12] can sit." 
The circuit court replied, "[Juror 12] has responded and stated 
that she believes she could be a fair and impartial juror," and "Queen's, as she indicated, is a large place." The circuit 
court then asked if there was any other basis to remove Juror 12 
for cause, to which Gramley's counsel responded, "No, Your 
Honor." Gramley presents no other grounds for which Juror 12 
should have been removed for cause.  See Baron, 80 Hawaiʻ i at 
114, 905 P.2d at 620. 
Gramley next asserts that the circuit court abused its 
discretion by denying her request to exercise a for-cause 
challenge at the bench. Relying on HRE Rules 103(c) and 104(c), 
Gramley asserts that the manner in which the for-cause challenge 
occurred created a "highly prejudicial situation for [her]."  But those rules are inapplicable here because no "evidence" was 
presented to the jury panel during voir dire.  Juror 12's 
statements were made in response to Gramley's voir dire and

(5) Finally, Gramley argues the circuit court abused 
its discretion by awarding costs to Queen's (POE 5). 
We review decisions regarding the taxation of costs 
under the abuse of discretion standard. Pulawa v. GTE Hawaiian 
Tel, 112 Hawaiʻ i 3, 10-11, 143 P.3d 1205, 1212-13 (2006). 
Here, Queen's sought to recover costs incurred in the 
course of litigation, including copying costs, deposition costs, and experts' expenses. The circuit court awarded costs pursuant 
to HRCP Rules 54 and 68. Gramley asks this court to consider that Queen's reported $2.1 billion in revenue in 2024 and that 
Gramley "is over 80 years old, disabled and living in a care 
home." 
HRCP 54(d)(1) creates a strong presumption that the 
prevailing party will recover costs, providing that "[e]xcept 
when express provision therefor is made either in a statute or 
in these rules, costs shall be allowed as of course to the 
prevailing party unless the court otherwise directs[.]" The 
losing party must overcome the presumption by "showing that an award would be inequitable under the circumstances." Wong v. 
Takeuchi
, 88 Hawaiʻi 46, 52, 961 P.2d 611, 617 (1998) (citation 
modified).  Moreover, "when costs are awardable to a prevailing 
16   NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAIʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER 
 
 
 
    
 
 
 
  
 
   
  
 
 party under HRCP Rule 54(d) and a particular taxable cost is 
allowed by statute or precedent, then actual disbursements for 
this purpose are presumptively reasonable. The adverse party 
has the burden of challenging the reasonableness of a particular 
cost request."  Id. at 53-54, 961 P.2d at 618-19. 
However, Gramley's opening brief does not challenge 
the reasonableness of any particular cost requested. 
Additionally, under HRCP Rule 68, "[i]f the judgment 
finally obtained by the offeree is not more favorable than the 
offer, the offeree must pay the costs incurred after the making of the offer." HRCP Rule 68 was designed "to encourage 
settlements and discourage vexatious suits and thus diminish the 
burden of litigation." Canalez v. Bob's Appliance Serv. Ctr., 
89 Hawaiʻ i 292, 306-09, 972 P.2d 295, 309-12 (1999) (citation 
modified) (holding that expert witness costs were properly taxed 
against a party whose refusal to settle under HRCP Rule 68 
necessitated a trial). 
Gramley rejected Queen's offer of settlement and 
ultimately failed to obtain a more favorable result at trial.  
Thus, under HRCP Rule 68, Queen's is also entitled to costs 
incurred after making the offer.
