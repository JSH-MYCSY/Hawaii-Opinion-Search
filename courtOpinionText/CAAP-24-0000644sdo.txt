 (3) Father contends that the family court's adoption 
of the January 10, 2024 Permanent Plan ( 2024 Permanent Plan ) was 
not in TA's best interest because the timelines set forth 
therein had already expired and thus could not have satisfied 
HRS §§ 587A -4 or -33 (2018). Father further contends that he 
was deprived of due process because the termination of his 
parental rights was "based on a written plan whose timelines 
[had] already expired and whose operative terms [were] 
effectively replaced by testimony at trial." 
The record reflects that the family court's Order 
Terminating Parental Rights adopted the 2024 Permanent Plan, as 
amended to specify that placement for adoption would occur by 
December 2024 , and to calendar a permanency hearing for October 
8, 2024. HRS § 587A -32(a)(2) (2018) provides that a permanent 
plan shall "[e]stablish a reasonable period of time by which the 
adoption or legal guardianship shall be finalized." The statute 
does not "mandate specific parameters for determining the 
reasonable time." In re J.F. , No. CAAP -24-0000424, 2025 WL 
101647, at *4 ( Haw. App. Jan. 15, 2025) (SDO). 
Nothing in HRS §§ 587A -4, -32, or -33 provide s that an 
expired timeline in a permanent plan makes the plan "legally 
deficient." Father's arguments lack merit.
