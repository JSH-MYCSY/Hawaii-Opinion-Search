(1) The Family Court entered the OFP pursuant to HRS

We review the Family Court's exercise of discretion as

Father's arguments based on Mother's credibility and the weight of the evidence are unavailing. "[A]n appellate court will not pass upon issues dependent upon the credibility of witnesses and the weight of the evidence; this is the province of the trier of fact." Fisher, 111 Hawai# i at 46, 137 P.3d at 360 
(quoting Doe, 95 Hawai# i at 190, 20 P.3d at 623). On this 
record, we cannot say that the court, having considered the DHS Report and all of the facts presented by Mother and Father, abused its discretion in concluding that the OFP in favor of Mother was necessary to prevent domestic abuse or a recurrence of abuse.

In any event, the res judicata argument lacks merit. 
"[T]he party asserting claim preclusion has the burden of proving three elements to establish that an action is barred: 1) there was a final judgment on the merits, 2) both parties are the same or are in privity with the same parties in the original suit, and 3) the claim decided in the original suit is identical with the one presented in the action in question." 
Priceline.com, Inc. v. 
Dir. of Tax'n, 144 Hawai# i 72, 82, 436 P.3d 1155, 1165 (2019) 
(citing E. Sav. Bank, FSB v. Esteban, 129 Hawai# i 154, 159, 296 
P.3d 1062, 1067 (2013)). 
Here, Father did not establish that there was a final 
judgment on the merits of Mother's prior allegation regarding the choking incident. Father asserts that during hearings on the Petition, the Family Court "acknowledged" and "confirm[ed]" that the allegations in Mother's Petition had already been litigated and "dismissed with prejudice." Not so. 
The Family Court merely 
acknowledged Father's testimony, not the merit of what he was saying. The Family Court made quite clear in ruling on the Petition that the alleged incidents in 2021 were "never adjudicated by this court." Father presented no contrary admissible evidence. Accordingly, claim preclusion did not bar the court's consideration of the choking incident in entering the OFP. 
For the reasons discussed above, the September 18, 2024 
Order for Protection entered by the Family Court of the First Circuit is affirmed.
