 The record reflects that Pennymac filed its 
foreclosure complaint ( Complaint ) against the Kahapeas on August 
12, 2022. After multiple extensions of time to serve the 
Complaint, on February 6, 2024 the Circuit Court granted Pennymac leave to serve the Complaint by certified mail. While such service was pending, the Kahapeas filed a March 1, 2024 "Notice of Appearance," and multiple filings on March 6, 2024, 
including a counterclaim. All of the March 1 and 6, 2024 
filings reflected an identical "P.O. Box 875" address as the Kahapeas' return address. On March 28, 2024, Pennymac filed a notice of service indicating that the Kahapeas had been served with the Complaint by certified mail at the same "P.O. Box 875" address, and attached certified mail receipts signed by the 
Kahapeas. 
On July 22, 2024, Pennymac filed its MSJ. Pennymac's 
"Notice of Hearing" attached to its MSJ reflected the Kahapeas 
were served "via U.S. Mail" at the same "P.O. Box 875" address. Pennymac's counsel's "Certificate of Service" certified that the Motion was served on the Kahapeas at the same P.O. Box address. 
The Circuit Court conducted a hearing on the MSJ on 
August 8, 2024, at which the Kahapeas did not appear. The Kahapeas did not file any opposition or papers responding to the MSJ. Following the hearing, the Circuit Court filed its September 26, 2024 Order Granting MSJ and Judgment, and the Kahapeas timely appealed. 
(1) In support of their contention regarding the 
Circuit Court's alleged "refusal" to allow them to participate in the MSJ hearing, the Kahapeas argue that "no certificate of service was provided" for the MSJ filed on July 22, 2024, and they "were never properly notified of the [MSJ] or the hearing date . . . depriv[ing] [them] of the opportunity to respond," in

 violation of Hawai ʻi Rules of Civil Procedure Rule 5 and due 
process requirements under the federal and state constitutions. 
Pennymac counters that it "properly gave notice of its MSJ to [the Kahapeas] via U.S. Mail to . . . the address [the Kahapeas] listed on their Counterclaim." 
Here, the record reflects that the MSJ contained a 
"Notice of Hearing" and "Certificate of Service" indicating service upon the Kahapeas "via U.S. Mail" at the P.O. Box 875 address. The record does not support the Kahapeas' claims of inadequate notification of the MSJ hearing or lack of a "certificate of service." The Kahapeas do not point to anything in the record pertinent to the MSJ hearing that supports their 
contention that the Circuit Court "refus[ed] to allow 
participation in the foreclosure hearing." See HRAP Rule 
28(b)(7). 
(2) The Kahapeas challenge the foreclosure for lack 
of "proof of valid and verified chain of title  as holder or 
standing to claim the property as a creditor to the transaction or agreement/ originating contract." 
The Kahapeas did not oppose the MSJ. Legal arguments 
raised for the first time on appeal are waived. See Hawaii
Ventures, LLC v. Otaka, Inc., 114 Hawai ʻi 438, 500, 164 P.3d 696, 
758 (2007) (citation omitted). 
Assuming arguendo the Kahapeas' challenge is not 
waived, "[a] lender is not required to prove the validity of 
every transfer in the chain of title before it may foreclose on 
a property." Bank of N.Y. Mellon v. Rumbawa, No. CAAP-15-0000024, 2016 WL 482170, at *2 (Haw. Feb. 4, 2016) (SDO). Pennymac provided evidence in its MSJ to establish its standing to foreclose, of an April 26, 2022 Bailee Letter reflecting Pennymac's counsel's receipt and possession of the original note

See Bank of Am., N.A. v. Reyes-Toledo, 139 Hawai ʻi 361, 390 P.3d

For the foregoing reasons, we affirm the September 26,
