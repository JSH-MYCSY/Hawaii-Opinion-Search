Upon careful review of the record and the briefs 
submitted by the parties, and having given due consideration to 
the arguments advanced and the issues raised, we resolve C.T.'s 
points of error as follows: 
(1, 2, 3, & 5) C.T. argues, variously, that the Family 
Court abused its discretion in entering the Order Denying Motion 
to Amend/Dissolve because his default in appearing at the 
November 9, 2021 hearing was due to technical difficulties, 
miscommunications, mistake and/or inadvertence. 
C.T. did
 not appeal the Protective Order or timely move 
to reconsider, alter or amend the order under Hawai i Family  
Court Rules (HFCR) Rule 59(e). On August 1, 2022, 265 days after 
the Protective Order was entered, C.T. filed a Motion to Set 
Aside Default Judgement Pursuant to Rule 60(b) (Rule 60(b) 
Motion). C.T. argued he was unfairly defaulted at the November 
9, 2021 evidentiary hearing because he attempted to attend the 
hearing by Zoom but was never admitted into the courtroom from

entitled to relief pursuant to HRS § 586-9. Nevertheless, in

protection to demonstrate that there has been a "material change 
in the circumstances of the parties since the issuance or last 
modification of the order" and expressly permits a family court 
to deny such motion without a hearing "if the motion, on its 
face, does not allege facts sufficient to establish a material
