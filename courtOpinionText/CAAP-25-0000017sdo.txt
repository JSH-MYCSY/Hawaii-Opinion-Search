 to the arguments advanced and the issues raised by the parties, 
we resolve Husband's contentions of error as follows: 
(1) We first address Husband's second, third, and 
fifth contentions of error, which directly challenge the family 
court's December 31, 2020 Divorce Decree . Husband's belated 
attempts to challenge the Divorce Decree , through an appeal that 
was taken on January 10, 2025, are untimely and therefore 
waived.3 See Order Denying October 16, 2006 Motion to Dismiss 
Appeal, & Order to Show Cause, Fox v. Fox , No. 28032, 2006 WL 
3040640, at *2 (Haw. App. Oct. 25, 2006 ) (concluding that the 
appellate court lacked jurisdiction to review the divorce decree 
where "[t]he time period for appealing [the divorce decree] 
ha[d] already expired under [Hawaiʻi Rules of Appellate 
Procedure] Rule 4(a)"); see also Cleveland v. Cleveland , 57 Haw. 
519, 524, 559 P.2d 744, 748 (1977) ("It would be intolerable for 
an unappealed decree terminating marriage to remain uncertain as 
to finality because the family court continues to retain 
jurisdiction of the proceeding to deal with [collateral 
matters].").

that the family court erred in denying his HFCR Rule 11 Motion

Motion. Husband's HFCR Rule 11 Motion argued that the Divorce 
Decree was invalid for lack of Husband's signature, and that the 
family court should therefore strike the Divorce Decree and 
impose monetary sanctions on Wife. Wife did not file a 
response. The family court heard argument from both parties on 
December 11, 2024.
