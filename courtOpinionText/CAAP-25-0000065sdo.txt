The Nices' opening brief does not comply with Hawai# i 
Rules of Appellate Procedure (HRAP) Rule 28(b). 
In particular, 
the opening brief does not contain "[a] concise statement of the points of error set forth in separately numbered paragraphs[,]" as required by HRAP Rule 28(b)(4). To promote access to justice, we do not automatically foreclose self-represented litigants from appellate review for not complying with court rules. Erum v. 
Llego, 147 Hawai# i 368, 380-81, 465 P.3d 815, 827-28 (2020). We 
address what we discern to be the Nices' arguments. 
(1) The Nices contend: "[W]e only wanted to file a TRO 
to give us time to figure out the next steps but the 5th Circuit Court wrongfully, fraudulently, and illegally blocked us from doing so and continue to refuse to correct it." 
In fact, the Nices initiated an action against 
Respondents by filing the complaint/request for preliminary injunction, designating themselves as "petitioner" and ASB and WI as "respondents." It is irrelevant that they wanted to file a form of action against Respondents styled as a "TRO" to stop a "fraudulent" foreclosure, where all they could allege was that the above-described demand letter had been sent to them. 
In any 
event, when the MTD was filed, the Nices did not seek to dismiss the action and they did not file an opposition to the MTD. Additionally, the record contains no hearing transcripts. The appellant has the burden to show error by reference to the record, and has the responsibility to provide adequate
