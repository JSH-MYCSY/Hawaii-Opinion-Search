We first note that Plotkins's opening brief does not 
comply with Hawaiʻ i Rules of Appellate Procedure (HRAP) Rule 28. 
Importantly, Plotkins does not present any points of error, corresponding arguments, or record citations. See HRAP 
Rules 28(b)(4), -(7) (requiring opening briefs to contain a "concise statement of the points of error set forth in 
separately numbered paragraphs" and an "argument, containing the 
contentions of the appellant . . . with citations to the 
authorities, statutes and parts of the record relied on"). 
Nonetheless, to promote access to justice, we 
liberally interpret a self-represented litigant's briefs and do not automatically foreclose them from appellate review because 
they fail to comply with court rules. Erum v. Llego, 147 Hawai‘ i 
368, 380-81, 465 P.3d 815, 827-28 (2020).  We thus address 
Plotkins's arguments to the extent we can discern. But, to the 
extent we can discern, Plotkins's arguments appear to challenge 
the circuit court's December 20, 2024 
Final Judgment over which 
we have no jurisdiction. 
In October 2022, the Department of Finance, County of 
Kaua
ʻi filed a complaint in the circuit court requesting sale of 
Plotkins's property at Lokelani Road in Kapaʻ a(the Lokelani 
Property
), as Plotkins owed the County of Kauaʻ i over $65,000.00

In September 2024, the commissioner appointed to sell

The circuit court confirmed the sale and ordered $90,609.85 be

On December 20, 2024, the circuit court entered the 
Final Judgment in favor of the County of Kauaʻ i and against 
Plotkins. Plotkins did not appeal from the Final Judgment. 
On January 31, 2025, the circuit court entered the 
Writ of Possession, ordering that Plotkins and his belongings be 
removed from the Lokelani Property. 
On February 24, 2025, 66 days after entry of the Final 
Judgment and 24 days after the entry of the Writ of Possession, 
Plotkins filed a notice of appeal. See 
HRAP Rule 4(a)(1) ("When 
a civil appeal is permitted by law, the notice of appeal shall

Plotkins stated he was appealing "from the 
Foreclosure Default Judgment, Writ of Possession, filed, herein on January 31

Exhibit A consisted only of the January 31, 2025 Writ of Possession. 
The County of Kauaʻ i moved to dismiss the appeal for 
lack of jurisdiction, explaining that Plotkins failed to timely 
appeal from the Final Judgment. 
This court denied the motion 
because Plotkins was appealing from the Writ of Possession, not the Final Judgment. 
Plotkins's opening brief, however, makes no 
discernible argument related to the Writ of Possession. Plotkins refers to the "[w]rongful foreclosure" and states that 
the County of 
Kauaʻi "does not have the authority to sell at less 
than appraised value any property." Plotkins further states, 
"The default judgment, is a travesty, and should be declared 
'Null and Void'." In his reply brief, Plotkins asserts, "The 
scope of the appeal cannot be limited to the writ of 
possession." We do not have jurisdiction to address arguments 
related to the Final Judgment. And because Plotkins does not
