Nnamdi Kassel, representing himself, appeals from the 
Judgment for Possession
 entered by the District Court of the 
Third Circuit, North and South Kona Division.1 We affirm. 
Joseph Kassel filed a complaint for summary possession 
against Nnamdi on June 17, 2024. The complaint stated: "There 
is no written rental agreement for the premises, only an oral agreement." Nnamdi entered a denial. Trial was conducted on November 27, 2024, and January 16 and February 20, 2025. The Judgment for Possession against Nnamdi was entered on February 27, 2025. This appeal followed. 
Nnamdi's opening brief does not comply with Hawai# i 
Rules of Appellate Procedure (HRAP) Rule 28(b). 
To promote 
access to justice, we do not automatically foreclose self-represented litigants from appellate review for not complying with court rules. Erum v. Llego, 147 Hawai# i 368, 380-81, 465

P.3d 815, 827-28 (2020). We address what we discern to be 
Nnamdi's arguments, but we disregard the exhibits to his brief that are not part of the record. HRAP Rule 28(b)(10) ("Anything that is not part of the record shall not be appended to the brief, except as provided in this Rule."). 
The opening brief makes representations about what 
happened during the trial, but the record contains no transcripts. The appellant has the burden to show error by reference to the record, and has the responsibility to provide adequate transcripts. Bettencourt v. Bettencourt, 80 Hawai# i 
225, 230, 909 P.2d 553, 558 (1995). The opening brief points to the district court's minutes, "but minutes are not evidence, and are solely for the court's use." State v. McKeown

(1) Nnamdi argues the district court "lacks 
jurisdiction over landlord-tenant summary possession actions, which must be brought in the Small Claims Landlord-Tenant Court." There is no "Small Claims Landlord-Tenant Court." See Hawaii Revised Statutes (HRS) § 666-6 (2016) ("In the case of summary 
possession proceedings, the person entitled to the possession of 
the premises shall bring and prosecute the person's action in the district court of the circuit wherein the lands and premises in question are situated."). The argument lacks merit.
(2) Nnamdi argues the complaint "did not allege a 
valid lease[.]" It alleged an oral month-to-month rental agreement. Oral leases of real property for any period not exceeding one year are valid. HRS § 666-4 (2016). 
Nnamdi argues the complaint "failed to attach the 
statutorily required 45-day written notice under HRS § 521-68." HRS § 521-68 (Supp. 2023) contains no 45-day notice requirement. 
Nnamdi cites various provisions of the Hawai# i
 Rules of 
Civil Procedure (HRCP). The HRCP do not apply to actions pending

in district court. See HRCP Rule 1(a) ("These Rules govern the 
procedure in the circuit courts of the State[.]").
(3) Nnamdi argues that Joseph "collected rent payments 
during June and July 2024 while simultaneously pursuing 
eviction," but cites no evidence in the record. We disregard the argument. HRAP Rule 28(b)(7). 
Nnamdi argues there was no "written and recorded 
Additional Farm Dwelling lease agreement" required by HRS § 205-4.5. HRS § 205-4.5 (2017 & Supp. 2023) contains no such requirement. 
Nnamdi argues "HRS § 521-21 prohibits landlords from 
demanding or retaining rent without a valid lease or statutory compliance." Joseph's complaint alleged an oral month-to-month rental agreement, and oral leases of real property for any period not exceeding one year are valid. HRS § 666-4. 
Nnamdi argues "HRS § 521-74 forbids retaliatory acts, 
including bad faith rent demands during eviction." He cites no evidence in the record showing retaliatory acts or bad faith rent demands. We disregard the argument. HRAP Rule 28(b)(7).
(4) Nnamdi argues the district court "abused its 
discretion by admitting Exhibits 8, 9, and 10 during rebuttal on February 20, 2025[.]" He cites Exhibit 11 to his opening brief, which is a copy of district court minutes and portions of unidentified, unauthenticated documents. The brief fails to comply with HRAP Rule 28(b)(4)(A) (requiring points of error to include, "when the point involves the admission or rejection of evidence, a quotation of the grounds urged for the objection and the full substance of the evidence admitted or rejected"). We disregard this argument. HRAP Rule 28(b)(4) ("Points not presented in accordance with this section will be disregarded[.]").
(5) Nnamdi argues the district court "improperly 
adjudicated title and ownership disputes within a landlord-tenant summary possession action[.]" He failed to order the trial transcripts necessary to show what was adjudicated. He also fails to cite to the record where he raised the issue. We are not obligated to search the record for information that should

have been provided by the appellant. Hawaii Ventures, LLC v. 
Otaka, Inc., 114 Hawai# i 438, 480, 164 P.3d 696, 738 (2007). We 
disregard this argument. HRAP Rule 28(b)(4).
(6) Nnamdi argues the Judgment for Possession and Writ 
of Possession were defective for noncompliance with 
Hawai#i 
Electronic Filing & Service Rules Rule 5.2. The rule applies to 
attorneys who file documents using the Judiciary Electronic Filing System. 
The Judgment and Writ were signed by the district 
court judge and conventionally served on Joseph and Nnamdi. There was no defect. 
Nnamdi argues "the case was marked 'TERMINATED' on 
February 20, 2025, without issuance of written Ruling, Order or Notice." The record contains no support for this argument.
(7) Nnamdi argues his "timely motions to Amend and 
Vacate under HRDCP [sic] rule 15(a), HRCP Rule 60(b)(1)-(4), (6) were pending when judgment enforcement proceeded, violating Appellant's right to be heard at a meaningful time in a meaningful manner." 
District Court Rules of Civil Procedure Rule 
15(a) pertains to amended and supplemental pleadings. The HRCP do not apply to cases in the district court. Nnamdi fails to cite to the record where his motions to amend and vacate, or their dispositions, appear. We disregard this argument. HRAP Rule 28(b)(4). 
The February 27, 2025 Judgment for Possession is 
affirmed.
