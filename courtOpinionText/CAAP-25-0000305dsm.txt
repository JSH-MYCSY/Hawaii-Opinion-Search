(By: Nakasone, Chief Judge, Leonard and McCullen, JJ.) 
Upon consideration of Plaintiff/Counterclaim 
Defendant-Appellant Lauren Lipcon's (Lipcon) June 5, 2026  
"Notice of Mootness and Motion to Dismiss Interlocutory Appeal" 
(Motion to Dismiss Appeal), the papers in support and in opposition, and the record, it appears that Lipcon seeks  NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAIʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER 
 
  
 
 
 
 
  dismissal of her appeal challenging the Circuit Court of the 
Second Circuit's April 14, 2025 "Order Denying [Lipcon]'s Motion to Compel Arbitration of Amended Counterclaim". 
Lipcon's appeal seeks to enforce arbitration of the 
Amended Counterclaim filed by Defendants/Counterclaimants-Appellees Maliko Coffee Cottages et al. (Maliko Coffee). After Lipcon appealed, the circuit court appears to have dismissed the Amended Counterclaim on its merits. With no Amended Counterclaim to arbitrate, Lipcon moves to dismiss her appeal as 
moot. See Lathrop v. Sakatani, 111 Hawaiʻ i 307, 312, 141 P.3d 
480, 485 (2006) ("Courts will not consume time deciding abstract 
propositions of law or moot cases, and have no jurisdiction to 
do so.") (citation omitted); County of Hawaiʻ i v. Ala Loop 
Homeowners, 123 Hawaiʻ i 391, 405, 253 P.3d 1103, 1117 (2010) ("A 
case is moot if it has lost its character as a present, live 
controversy" and "if the reviewing court can no longer grant effective relief.") (citations omitted). 
Arbitrability "is easily severable from the merits of 
the underlying disputes." Moses H. Cone Mem'l Hosp. v. Mercury Constr. Corp., 460 U.S. 1, 20-21 (1983); Motorola Credit Corp. v. Uzan, 388 F.3d 39, 53 (2nd Cir. 2004) ("The issue, therefore, 
is whether the trial of a case on the merits is 'involved in' an 
appeal of an order denying arbitration. The only authority in this Circuit strongly suggests that it is not."); Britton v. Co-

Because the Amended Counterclaim upon which the appeal 
is based has been dismissed, and because Maliko Coffee may not appeal from the circuit court's refusal to allow an appeal from its interlocutory order dismissing the Amended Counterclaim, we 
elect to dismiss the appeal pursuant to Hawaiʻ i Rules of 
Appellate Procedure Rule 42(b). 
Therefore, IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that the Motion to 
Dismiss Appeal is granted.
