On appeal, Choi appears to contend that the District 
Court erred by: 
(1) determining that service of process was 
improper; (2) concluding that the court lacked jurisdiction over her claim; (3) violating District Court Rules of Civil Procedure (DCRCP) 56(c), (d), (f), and (g), and depriving Choi of her right 
to sue "due to [the court's] personal feelings and bias against

foreigners"; and (4) denying Choi's motion for reconsideration, 
"acting on a prejudicial basis and without review[.]"2/ 
The State contends that Choi filed an untimely notice 
of appeal, and this court therefore lacks jurisdiction. Otherwise, the State is not contesting service of process, but argues that the District Court properly concluded that it lacked subject matter jurisdiction over Choi's claim. 
After reviewing the record on appeal and the relevant 
legal authorities, and giving due consideration to the issues raised and the arguments advanced by the parties, we resolve Choi's contentions as follows, and affirm. 
(1) Appellate Jurisdiction. We first address the 
State's contention that this court lacks jurisdiction over Choi's appeal. The State argues that the notice of appeal was untimely, as it was filed on May 15, 2025, more than 30 days after entry of the April 9, 2025 Dismissal Order, in violation of HRAP Rule 4(a). The State further argues that Choi's May 1, 2025 Request to Set Aside Entry of [Dismissal Order]  (Request to Set Aside), 
even if considered a motion for relief under DCRCP 60(b), was not a tolling motion that extended the time for filing the notice of appeal under HRAP Rule 4(a)(3). 
We construe the Request to Set Aside as, in part, a 
motion for relief under DCRCP Rule 60(b). The operative language of DCRCP Rule 60(b) is identical to that of 
Hawai#i Rules of 
Civil Procedure (HRCP) Rule 60(b). In Mâlama Kakanilua v. Dir. 
of Dep't of Pub. Works, 157 Hawai# i 280, 576 P.3d 793 (2025), the 
supreme court held that "for purposes of serving as a 'tolling 
motion,' an HRCP Rule 60(b) motion extends the time in which to file a notice of appeal pursuant to HRAP Rule 4(a)(3), if the motion for reconsideration is filed within thirty days of the entry of judgment." Id. at 293-94, 576 P.3d at 806-07.

Here, Choi's Request to Set Aside was filed within 30 
days of entry of the Dismissal Order. As a DCRCP Rule 60(b) 
motion, it was a tolling motion under HRAP Rule 4(a)(3), which extended the time for filing the notice of appeal until 30 days after entry of the May 5, 2025 denial of the Request to Set Aside. The May 15, 2025 notice of appeal was therefore timely under HRAP Rule 4(a)(3), and this court has jurisdiction over Choi's appeal. 
(2) The Dismissal Order. Choi appears to contend that 
the District Court erred in dismissing her complaint where (a) "the deceased . . . was already receiving benefits after being approved for 'unemployment benefit eligibility' . . . so HRS [chapter] 383 did not apply[,]" and (b) the case "was a civil action under [Hawaii Revised Statutes (HRS)] § 708-830[ ](Theft) 
. . . ." 
"Our review of a motion to dismiss for lack of subject 
matter jurisdiction is based on the contents of the complaint, 
the allegations of which we accept as true and construe in the light most favorable to the plaintiff." Casumpang v. ILWU, LocalCasumpang v. ILWU, Local 142, 94 
Hawai#i 330, 337, 13 P.3d 1235, 
1242 (2000) (brackets omitted) (quoting Norris v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc., 74 Haw. 235, 240, 842 P.2d 634, 637 (1992)). "[W]hen considering a motion to dismiss pursuant to Rule 12(b)(1) the trial court is not restricted to the face of the pleadings, but may  review any evidence, such as affidavits and testimony, to 
resolve factual disputes concerning the existence of jurisdiction." Id. at 337, 13 P.3d at 1242 (original brackets omitted, emphasis added) (quoting Norris, 74 Haw. at 240, 842 P.2d at 637). 
In her complaint, Choi alleged that the State owed 
money to her for "[d]eceased Michael K. Yoshida's unemployment [b]enefits unpaid (part)." 
She did not present any evidence in 
response to the State's motion to dismiss the complaint due in part to lack of jurisdiction. 
The Hawai#i Employment Security Law, HRS chapter 383, 
governs unemployment benefits. Claims for benefits must be made in accordance with regulations prescribed by the Department of

Labor and Industrial Relations. See HRS § 383-32; Haw. Admin. 
Rules (HAR) § 12-5-81. The claimant may file an appeal from the 
claim determination to the employment security appeals referee's 
office. See HRS § 383-38; HAR § 12-5-93. The claimant may then obtain judicial review of the referee's decision "in the manner provided in [HRS] chapter 91, by instituting proceedings in the circuit court of the circuit in which the claimant resides or in which the claimant was last employed." HRS § 383-41 (emphasis added). In sum, the Hawai# i Employment Security Law mandates 
exhaustion of administrative remedies before the referee and then channels judicial review exclusively to the circuit court under HRS § 383-41. 
Choi cites no authority supporting her contention that 
the process set forth in HRS chapter 383 does not apply to her claim because "the deceased . . . was already receiving benefits after being approved for 'unemployment benefit eligibility.'" We have found none. And HRS § 708-830, which defines the criminal offense of theft, does not provide a vehicle for suing the State in these circumstances. 
Accordingly, the District Court did not err in 
dismissing the complaint. 
(3) Remaining Contentions. Choi contends that the 
District Court violated DCRCP rules regarding summary judgment motions. However, the District Court dismissed the complaint based on the State's motion to dismiss for improper service and lack of jurisdiction. The case did not involve summary judgment, and the record does not support Choi's assertion that the court granted the State's motion to dismiss "due to [the court's] personal feelings and bias against foreigners." 
Choi contends that the District Court erred in denying 
her Request to Set Aside, which we have construed in part as a motion for relief under DCRCP Rule 60. There was no abuse of discretion in denying the motion, and the record does not support Choi's assertion that the court "act[ed] on a prejudicial basis and without review[.]"
