SUMMARY DISPOSITION ORDER 
(By: Leonard, Presiding Judge, Hiraoka and Wadsworth, JJ.) 
William Gilroy was found guilty of driving without a 
license and not having motor vehicle insurance. Representing 
himself, he appeals from the Judgment and Notice of Entry of 
Judgment
 entered by the District Court of the Third Circuit, Kona 
Division.1 We affirm. 
On November 18, 2024, Gilroy was issued a Citation for 
Traffic Crime(s) for Driving Motor Vehicle Without Valid Driver's 
License (Hawaii Revised Statutes (HRS) § 286-102) and No Motor 
Vehicle Insurance Policy (HRS § 431:10C-104). Trial was held on 
April 24 and May 23, 2025. Gilroy was found guilty. The Judgment was entered on May 23, 2025. This appeal followed. 
Gilroy's opening brief doesn't comply with Hawai# i 
Rules of Appellate Procedure (HRAP) Rule 28(b), but we do not 
automatically foreclose self-represented litigants from appellate 
review for not complying with court rules. Erum v. Llego
, 147

Hawai#i 368, 380-81, 465 P.3d 815, 827-28 (2020). We address 
what we discern to be Gilroy's arguments.
(1) Gilroy contends the statutes he violated apply 
only to persons engaged in commercial or regulated activities, 
not to private travelers like him. They apply to everyone. State v. French, 77 Hawai# i 222, 230, 883 P.2d 644, 652 (App. 
1994). 
Gilroy argues the district court lacked standing to 
proceed against him. "Standing is concerned with whether the parties have the right to bring suit." Pele Def. Fund v. Puna 
Geothermal Venture, 77 Hawai# i 64, 67, 881 P.2d 1210, 1213 (1994) 
(emphasis added). 
Gilroy argues that requiring a driver's license and 
motor vehicle insurance violates his right to travel on public roads under the federal and Hawai# i constitutions. They do not. 
French, 77 Hawai# i at 232, 883 P.2d at 654. 
Gilroy argues the district court prevented him from 
obtaining discovery. He does not cite to the record where the claimed error took place, as required by HRAP Rule 28(b)(4) and (7). We are not obligated to search the record for information that should have been provided by Gilroy. Hawaii 
Ventures, LLC v. Otaka, Inc., 114 Hawai# i 438, 480, 164 P.3d 696, 
738 (2007). We decline to address his argument. 
Gilroy argues the district court made several trial 
errors, but hasn't ordered trial transcripts for the record on appeal. 
"The burden is upon appellant in an appeal to show error 
by reference to matters in the record, and he or she has the responsibility of providing an adequate transcript." Bettencourt v. Bettencourt, 80 Hawai# i 225, 230, 909 P.2d 553, 558 (1995) 
(brackets omitted). 
We decline to address his argument.
(2) Gilroy argues that criminalizing "private, non-
commercial movement on public roadways absent harm or contractual duty exceeds the statute's authority and infringes" his fundamental right to travel. 
The authority he cites acknowledges 
the right to travel between states, but Gilroy wasn't traveling interstate. The authority he cites does not support the proposition that requiring a driver's license and motor vehicle

insurance unduly burdens his right to intrastate travel. It does 
not. French, 77 Hawai# i at 231-32, 883 P.2d at 653-54. 
Gilroy argues his due process rights were violated by: 
proceeding without a plea or lawful arraignment; ignoring jurisdictional and standing objections raised on the record; preventing him from questioning witnesses or the court; refusing to accept and review a key witness affidavit; and shifting the burden of proof to him, presuming guilt. He provides no citations to the record or transcripts of proceedings. We decline to address his arguments. HRAP Rule 28(b)(4) and (7).
(3) Gilroy argues the fine, fees, and driver's license 
suspension imposed by the district court were improper because the court lacked jurisdiction to impose them, and he never had a driver's license to suspend. The district court has jurisdiction over violations of HRS § 286-102. See HRS § 286-136 (Supp. 
2024) & 604-8(a) (2016). The district court also has jurisdiction over violations of HRS § 431:10C-104. See HRS 
§§ 431:10C-117(a) (Supp. 2024) & 604-8(a). The district court's suspension of a driver's license Gilroy claims to have never had would be harmless error. 
The May 23, 2025 Judgment and Notice of Entry of 
Judgment is affirmed. Gilroy's motion for retention of oral argument is denied.
