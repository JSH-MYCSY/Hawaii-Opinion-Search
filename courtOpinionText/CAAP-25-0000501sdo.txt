3 stating that the [circuit] court did not have jurisdiction since

Upon careful review of the record, briefs, and 
relevant legal authorities, and having given due consideration 
to the arguments advanced and the issues raised by the parties, 
we resolve AI1 Bonding's  contention of error as follows : 
As relevant here, HRS § 804 -51 provides that, upon the 
circuit court's forfeiture of a bond in a criminal case, 
judgment shall be entered "in favor of the State and against the 
. . . surety."  The circuit court , 
shall cause execution to issue thereon immediately after 
the expiration of thirty days from the date that notice is 
given via personal service or certified mail, return 
receipt request ed, to the surety . . . on the bond, of the 
entry of the judgment in favor of the State, unless before 
the expiration of thirty days from the date that notice is 
given to the surety . . . on the bond of the entry of the 
judgment in favor of the State, a motion or application of 
the . . . surety . . . , showing good cause why execution 
should not issue upon the judgment, is filed with the 
court. 
 
HRS § 804 -51 (emphasis added).  
The record reflects that AI1 Bonding's Motion to Set 
Aside was untimely filed on May 6, 2025  -- i.e., forty-one days 
after it was served with the Forfeiture Order/Judgment  by 
certified mail .  HRS § 804-51's thirty -day window for filing a NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAI‘I REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER  
 
4 motion to set aside the forfeiture constitutes a  jurisdictional 
requirement that is not waivable by the circuit court.  See 
State v. Vaimili , 131 Hawaiʻi 9, 15, 313 P.3d 698, 704 (2013) 
("[O]nce a bond is forfeited pursuant to HRS § 804 -51, a surety 
has thirty days from the time it receives notice of forfeiture 
to set aside the forfeiture judgment[.]"); State v. Ranger Ins. 
Co., 83 Hawaiʻi 118, 124 n.5, 925 P.2d 288, 294 n.5 (1996) 
(finding that the surety's motion "was not filed within the time 
limit imposed by HRS § 804 -51, and the circuit court was 
therefore without power to consider it."  (citation omitted) ). 
On this record, we conclude that the circuit court 
correctly determined that it lacked the power to consider  AI1 
Bonding's untimely Motion to Set Aside.   See State v. Deplae , 
No. CAAP -24-0000492, 2026 WL 1683929 , at *2 (Haw. App. June 10, 
2026) (SDO).  We therefore affirm the June 9, 2025  Order.
