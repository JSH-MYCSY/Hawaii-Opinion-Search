In the current appeal, the State presents three points

Upon careful review of the record and the briefs 
submitted by the parties and having given due consideration to 
the arguments advanced and the issues raised, we vacate the 
April 2025 Order and Judgment and remand for further 
proceedings. 
Points of Error 1-2:  In the 2018 Stipulation, the 
parties agreed (and the Circuit Court ordered) that the parties would be "bound by the rulings of [the Circuit Court] which are 
not reversed or vacated by an appellate court[.]" In ruling on 
Williams' 2017 MIL, the Circuit Court had ruled that Hallstrom 
(the State's expert) "may testify as a valuation witness for the

 State. Mr. Hallstrom's testimony, as proffered by the State in 
his deposition, is not irrelevant and its probative value is not 
outweighed by undue prejudice." That ruling was neither 
reversed nor vacated by an appellate court. Based on the plain 
language of the parties' 2018 Stipulation, Williams remained bound by that ruling on remand. See Provident Funding Assocs.,  
L.P. v. Gardner, 149 Hawaiʻ i 288, 296, 488 P.3d 1267, 1275 (2021) 
("Parties have leeway to bind themselves in a stipulation, and 
such agreements will be broadly enforced[.]").  Because the 2018 
Stipulation bars Williams from arguing the Hallstrom report 
should be excluded, the Circuit Court erred by excluding it. 
Thus, when considering the 2018 Stipulation, Williams' MSJ 
failed to show that the State's evidence was inadmissible; given 
the competing valuations from Williams and the State, Williams' 
MSJ failed to show the absence of a genuine issue of material 
fact, and the burden never shifted to the State. The Circuit Court thus erred in granting Williams' MSJ.  
Point of Error 3: The State argues that the Circuit 
Court erred in ruling that the income capitalization method was 
appropriate to determine just compensation, and that the 
comparable sales method should be used instead. 
Williams argues 
that because Williams I 
held that the Circuit Court abused its 
discretion by excluding evidence of possible future lease income, the law of the case is that the income capitalization 
method applies; Williams further contends that valuations based 
6

 on an unencumbered fee simple property are constitutionally 
inadmissible, because (i) the Property was encumbered (by the 
1994 Lease) and (ii) the act of condemnation removed the 
encumbrance, but (iii) the act of condemnation cannot affect the 
fair market value paid to the landowner. 
In Williams I we stated, "'Perhaps the most important 
consideration in the valuation of income-producing property is the anticipated income from that property.'" 2024 WL 1623547, 
at *4 (quoting City & Cnty. of Honolulu v. Mkt. Place, Ltd., 55 
Haw. 226, 243, 517 P.2d 7, 20 (1973)). We held that "a future 
income stream from leasing the Property would not be speculative 
because Williams had received actual income from leasing the Property and appraisal of the leased interest could have been 
presented to a jury." Id. Therefore, the Circuit Court 
correctly denied the State's MPSJ: evidence of valuation based on the income-capitalization method was admissible pursuant to 
Williams I. This does not necessarily mean that evidence of 
comparable sales is per se inadmissible, however. Once again, 
the 2018 Stipulation controls: the ruling on Williams' 2017 MIL 
was neither reversed nor vacated on appeal, such that 
Hallstrom's testimony is admissible.  Furthermore, as Williams' counsel stated to the Circuit Court in oral argument on his MSJ, 
"There is no one correct valuation method for every case." 
Neither Williams I nor today's summary disposition order holds 
that the comparable sales method was invalid, or that it could

 not be used as evidence of the Property's value (particularly, 
the Property's reversionary value at the conclusion of the 1994 
Lease). If an appraisal relied upon sales of similarly 
encumbered property, a jury could find the appraisal was 
reasonable. Differences in encumbrances would go to weight rather than admissibility. 
Therefore, we vacate the Circuit Court's April 2, 2025 
Order and July 9, 2025 Final Judgment and remand for further 
proceedings consistent with this summary disposition order.
