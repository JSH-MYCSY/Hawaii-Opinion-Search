 Mother has a fundamental liberty interest in the 
right of care, custody, and control of the Children, and 
thus "the State may not deprive her of this interest 
without providing a fair procedure for deprivation."  Doe 
v. Doe, 120 Hawaiʻ i 149, 168, 202 P.3d 610, 629 (App. 2009) 
(citations omitted).  "At its core, procedural due process 
of law requires notice and an opportunity to be heard at a 
meaningful time and in a meaningful manner before 
governmental deprivation of a significant liberty 
interest."  Id. (emphasis added) (quoting State v. Bani, 97 
Hawaiʻi 285, 293, 36 P.3d 1255, 1263 (2001)). 
In re TC, 150 Hawaiʻi 328, 500 P.3d 513, No. CAAP-21-0000053, 
2021 WL 6054831, at *3 (App. Dec. 20, 2021) (SDO). 
"The due process floor entails 'notice and an 
opportunity to be heard at a meaningful time and in a meaningful 
manner.'"  In re JH, 152 Hawaiʻi 373, 380-81, 526 P.3d 350, 357-
58 (2023) (quoting In re Doe, 99 Hawaiʻ i 522, 533, 57 P.3d 447, 
458 (2002)).  "Context shapes the process that is due."  Id. 
Here, Mother's attorney stated at the July 15, 2025 
Hearing that he had not received service of the July 7, 2025 
Updated Permanent Plan, despite the Certificate of Service 
indicating that it was mailed on July 7, 2025. 
Under the Hawai‘ i Family Court Rules (HFCR), "[s]ervice 
by mail is complete upon mailing."  HFCR Rule 5(c)(3).  However, "[a] party who has been prejudiced by failure to receive due 
notice or to be served, or who has been prejudiced by reason that service was made by mail, may apply to the court for appropriate relief."  HFCR Rule 5(d). 
Viewed in context, the family court's decision to go 
forward with the July 15, 2025 Hearing and to receive the NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAIʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER

 July 7, 2025 Updated Permanent Plan into evidence — despite 
Mother not having had an opportunity to review it with her attorney beforehand — violated Mother's due process rights, as her opportunity to be heard was not meaningful without reviewing 
the Plan.  See In re JH, 152  at 380-81, 526 P.3d at 357- Hawaiʻ
i
58; In re TC, 2021 WL 6054831, at *3. 
Thus, the family court abused its discretion by not 
providing Mother an opportunity to review the July 7, 2025 
Updated Permanent Plan before receiving it into evidence at the 
July 15, 2025 Hearing.

 We turn next to POE C, where Mother contends the 
family court improperly received the July 7, 2025 Updated Permanent Plan into evidence because DHS did not offer the preparer of the Plan for cross-examination as required under 
Hawaiʻi Revised Statutes (HRS) § 587A-18(d) (2018).  Mother makes 
the same arguments regarding the July 3, 2025 Safe Family Home Report. 
HRS § 587A-18(d) provides, "A written report submitted 
pursuant to this section shall be admissible and relied upon to 
the extent of its probative value in any proceeding under this 
chapter, subject to the right of any party to examine or cross-examine the preparer of the report."  (Emphasis added.)  HRS 
chapter 587A does not define the term "preparer of the report."  See HRS § 587A-4 (2018). NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAIʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER  
 
 
12 
 "[A]dmission of a report into evidence under HRS 
§ 587A-18(d) is not contingent upon actual cross examination of 
the preparer of the report, however such admission subjects the preparer of a report to cross examination."  In re TC, 2021 WL 
6054831, at *4.  DHS may call as a witness an employee other 
than the preparer of the report, so long as the preparer was not 
subpoenaed or otherwise compelled to attend the hearing.  E.g., 
In re EAC, 152 Hawaiʻ i 192, 524 P.3d 383, No. CAAP-22-0000381, 
2023 WL 1462794, at *3 (App. Feb. 2, 2023) (SDO) ("DHS listed Fitzpatrick, not Dosland, on its witness list for the contested case hearing.  The record does not indicate that Father or Mother subpoenaed Dosland or otherwise moved to compel her attendance to testify at the contested case hearing.  The family court does not abuse its discretion by not requiring testimony from a witness who was not listed, called, subpoenaed, or otherwise required to appear at the contested case hearing." 
(citation omitted)). 
Here, Robinson is properly considered the preparer of 
the July 7, 2025 Updated Permanent Plan, because Millon 
testified the Plan "was prepared by Wendy Robinson, as well as 
our staff in the office"; "Robinson is the main signature on there"; and Robinson "is the writer" of the "new material in the report[.]"  (Emphasis added.)  Millon admittedly "didn't read all of the report because it is not mine"; "vaguely looked over NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAIʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER  
 
 
13 
 the report"; and "briefly" discussed the case with Robinson for 
"about 10 minutes."  Millon also testified that she was standing in for Robinson while Robinson was on leave and that Robinson would have been "available at another date[.]" 
As such, the family court's admission of the July 7, 
2025 Updated Permanent Plan subjected Robinson, as the preparer of the report, to cross-examination.  See In re TC, 2021 WL 
6054831, at *4.  Nothing in the record suggests DHS gave Mother notice of its intent to offer Millon in Robinson's stead.  See 
In re EAC, 2023 WL 1462794, at *3.  Thus, the family court 
should have permitted Mother the opportunity to cross-examine Robinson as the preparer of the July 7, 2025 Updated Permanent Plan, and its failure to do so was error.  E.g., In re TC, 2021 
WL 6054831, at *4 ("[T]he Family Court did not err by admitting into evidence DHS's Permanent Plan filed on November 23, 2020; however, admission of this report meant that Mother should have been allowed to examine or cross-examine those who prepared that report or any DHS report admitted and relied upon by the Family Court.").

 To reiterate, "procedural due process of law requires 
notice and an opportunity to be heard at a meaningful time and 
in a meaningful manner before governmental deprivation of a significant liberty interest."  Id. at *3 (citation modified).  
And, as this court has explained, guardianship implicates a 
parent's fundamental liberty interest: 
However, although a guardianship may not absolutely 
and irrevocably supersede all the rights of a parent, it 
certainly supersedes many of these parental rights, such as the right to the custody of and to provide care for the child.  See HRS § 560:5-208(a) (2018) (" Except as otherwise 
limited by the court, a guardian of a minor ward has the 
powers of a parent regarding the ward's support, care, 
education, health, and welfare." ); 39 Am. Jur. 2d Guardian 
and Ward § 90 (2025) (" Until the guardianship of a minor 
child is terminated, the guardian's right to custody of the minor is superior to that of the minor's parent." ).  
Therefore, the fundamental liberty interest of a parent in the care, custody, and control of their child is implicated in guardianship proceedings. 
In re Guardianship of I.W., 157 Hawaiʻi 437, 579 P.3d 899, 
Nos. CAAP-23-0000356 and CAAP-23-0000598, 2025 WL 3215249, 
at *15 (App. Nov. 18, 2025) (mem. op.). 
Here, the family court ordered that the July 7, 2025 
Updated Permanent Plan be implemented, which included legal guardianship of the Children without termination of parental rights.  Awarding legal guardianship to the Children's paternal grandparents constitutes a significant infringement upon 
Mother's fundamental liberty interest in the right of care, 
custody, and control of the Children, even if her parental rights were not terminated.  See, e.g., id. NOT FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAIʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER  
 
 
15 
 Because Mother was not afforded an opportunity "to be 
heard at a meaningful time and in a meaningful manner" through 
an evidentiary hearing on her changed circumstances prior to the family court's ruling, the family court erred.  See In re TC, 
2021 WL 6054831, at *3 (emphasis omitted) (quoting Doe, 120 
Hawaiʻi at 168, 202 P.3d at 629). 
Based on the foregoing, we vacate the family court's 
August 8, 2025 Order and remand the case for proceedings 
consistent with this summary disposition order.
