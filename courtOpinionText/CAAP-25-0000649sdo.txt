SUMMARY DISPOSITION ORDER 
(By: Hiraoka, Presiding Judge, McCullen and Guidry, JJ.) 
John Dragomanovich filed a Petition for Ex Parte 
Temporary Restraining Order and for Injunction Against Harassment 
against Heather Boyd in the District Court of the First Circuit, 
Honolulu Division. A trial was held. The district court granted 
the petition. The Injunction Against Harassment was entered on 
September 12, 2025.
1 Boyd, representing herself, appeals. We 
affirm. 
Boyd's opening brief does not comply with Hawai# i Rules 
of Appellate Procedure (HRAP
) Rule 28(b). To promote access to 
justice, we do not automatically foreclose her from appellate review. Erum v. Llego, 147 Hawai# i 368, 380-81, 465 P.3d 815, 
827-28 (2020). However, her brief argues error without citing to the record, as required by HRAP Rule 28(b)(4) and (7). We are not obligated to search the record for information that should have been provided by the appellant. Hawaii Ventures, LLC v. 
Otaka, Inc.
, 114 Hawai# i 438, 480, 164 P.3d 696, 738 (2007).

Boyd argues trial error but she failed to include the 
trial transcript in the record on appeal, as required by HRAP 
Rule 10(b)(1)(A). "The burden is upon appellant in an appeal to show error by reference to matters in the record, and he or she has the responsibility of providing an adequate transcript." Bettencourt v. Bettencourt, 80 Hawai# i 225, 230, 909 P.2d 553, 
558 (1995) (brackets omitted). 
Boyd makes legal arguments without citing authority, 
as required by HRAP Rule 28(b)(7). She challenges the district court's jurisdiction based on "double jeopardy." "Double jeopardy protects individuals against: (1) a second prosecution for the same offense after acquittal; (2) a second prosecution for the same offense after conviction; and (3) multiple punishments for the same offense." State v. Kalua, 144 Hawai# i 
7, 11, 434 P.3d 1202, 1206 (2019). The proceeding below was a civil action in which double jeopardy did not apply. The district court has jurisdiction to enjoin and temporarily restrain harassment. 
HRS § 604-10.5 (2016 & Supp. 2024). 
"The law is clear in this jurisdiction that the 
appellant has the burden of furnishing the appellate court with a sufficient record to positively show the alleged error." Bettencourt, 80 Hawai# i at 230, 909 P.2d at 558. Boyd did not 
sustain her burden. 
The district court's September 12, 2025 Injunction 
Against Harassment is affirmed. Boyd's June 8, 2026 motion is denied.
