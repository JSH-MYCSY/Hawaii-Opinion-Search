SUMMARY DISPOSITION ORDER  
(By: McCullen, Presiding Judge, Guidry and Gluck, JJ.) 
Respondent-Appellant Sarita Knull appeals the October   
16, 2025 Order For Protection (
OFP) entered by the Family Court 
of the Third Circuit (Family Court).   Knull's single point of 
error is that the OFP was a "mistake" because "the court was 
clearly erroneous and abused its discretion in finding the testimonies of Petitioner-Appellee [Nicole Robinson-Wells] and [eyewitness] Joy Modisett credible" (formatting altered).   Upon 
careful review of the record and the briefs submitted by Knull, 
and having given due consideration to the matters presented  
therein, we affirm.   1

 Following an evidentiary hearing at which both 
Robinson-Wells and Modisett testified (but Knull did not), the 
Family Court stated:  
[A]fter full consideration of the credible and 
reliable evidence in this case, the Court first notes that 
basically there are two witnesses giving eyewitness 
testimony, that being the Petitioner and Ms. Modisett. The 
Court finds that their -- their testimony, though differing 
in certain aspects, is essentially consistent, and the 
Court finds Ms. Modisett's testimony credible. 
In her Opening Brief, Knull points to alleged inconsistencies 
between Modisett's and Robinson-Wells' testimonies; she argues that "[t]hese two versions are so markedly different" that the Family Court could not have found their testimony to be "essentially consistent" or found Modisett's testimony to be credible. Knull also argues that the Family Court erred in failing to consider the testimony of another witness, Perrietta 
Edwards-Turner (the parties' aunt), who testified as a rebuttal 
witness that Robinson-Wells "said that she was going after [Knull] because . . . [Knull] had a lot of money."  
At the October 16, 2025 evidentiary hearing, Modisett 
testified as follows: 
[Knull]  suddenly charged at [Robinson- Wells]  and began 
pushing and  kicking and punching her in rapid succession. 
[Robinson- Wells]  -- at that point, [Robinson- Wells]  was 
still holding the dog secure in  her arms, but when [Knulls]  
found out that she couldn't  wrestle him away from her, she 
body -- body-slammed  [Robinson- Wells], causing her to 
stumble backwards.  
The Family Court found Modisett's testimony to be credible. "It is well-settled that an appellate court will not pass upon issues dependent upon the credibility of witnesses and the weight of the evidence; this is the province of the trier of 
fact."  In re Doe
, 95 Hawaiʻi 183, 190, 20 P.3d 616, 623 (2001) 
(cleaned up). This court will not disturb the Family Court's 
determination that Modisett was credible.

 Further, Knull's assertion that the Family Court 
failed to consider the testimony of Edwards-Turner is without merit. Before making its credibility findings, the Family Court expressly stated that it has "considered the testimony of the parties and -- well, the testimony of the Petitioner and other witnesses as well as Respondent's rebuttal witness," referring to Edwards-Turner.  
Knull does not explain how the Family Court's issuance 
of the OFP was an abuse of discretion given Modisett's credible testimony. The record contains substantial evidence — specifically, Modisett's credible testimony — to support the Family Court's determination that Robinson-Wells proved the material allegations of abuse, by a preponderance of the evidence, underlying her request for an OFP.  Knull has not provided a cognizable argument that the Family Court abused its discretion in issuing the OFP. 
Knull argues that the Family Court's credibility 
findings were clearly erroneous and constituted an abuse of discretion, and that the Family Court made a "mistake" in 
issuing the OFP.  As the Hawaiʻ i Supreme Court has explained: 
The family court's [findings of fact] are reviewed on 
appeal under the "clearly erroneous" standard. A [finding 
of fact] is clearly erroneous when (1) the record lacks 
substantial evidence to support the finding, or (2) despite 
substantial evidence in support of the finding, the appellate court is nonetheless left with a definite and 
firm conviction that a mistake has been made. "Substantial 
evidence" is credible evidence which is of sufficient 
quality and probative value to enable a person of 
reasonable caution to support a conclusion. 
Id. (cleaned up). Here, we are not "left with a definite and 
firm conviction that a mistake has been made." Id.
