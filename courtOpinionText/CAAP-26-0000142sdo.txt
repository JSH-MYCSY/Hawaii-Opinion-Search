Section II., FOFs 46, 50, 51. The family court's FOF 41 – that Parents failed to present evidence of stable housing capable of supporting the Child's need for oxygen – is not an improper shifting of the burden of proof. Rather, it is a finding, after DHS introduced evidence raising safety concerns about the Child, that the family court did not find Parents' evidence, consisting primarily of Father's testimony, either credible or persuasive.

B. Father's Contentions  
Father joins Mother's contentions. For the reasons 
stated above, those contentions fail and Father's effort to 
piggyback on them likewise fails. Father additionally challenges Section II., FOF 52, along with COLs 12 and 14, and Section III., FOF 2. 
As to FOF 52 and COLs 12 and 14, Father contends that 
the record lacks substantial evidence to support the findings that DHS made reasonable efforts (a) to prevent or eliminate the need for removal of the Child from the family home, and (b) to finalize the permanency plan, which was reunification. Father argues that DHS did not make reasonable efforts to unify Father with the Child and did not provide Father a reasonable opportunity for reunification. 
Substantial evidence supports the challenged FOFs and 
COLs, which present mixed issues of fact and law. That evidence, as reflected in numerous unchallenged FOFs, includes the following: The day after the Child was born, DHS referred the family to Family Strengthening Services upon receiving a report of concern for the Child. Section II., FOF 3. When Father identified paternal grandmother as a proposed safety plan participant, DHS spoke with paternal grandmother and requested her identification to perform background clearances, which was

HRS § 587A-5 states: 
Pursuant to section 571-11(9), the court shall have

(1) In a child protective proceeding concerning any
