(7) Notwithstanding the many caveats, including that 
documents submitted to the Third Circuit using the Document 
Drop-off service are not "officially filed" unless court staff electronically file them, the Document Drop-off service is presented by the Judiciary as a "convenient alternative" to submitting documents to the Third Circuit conventionally. In this case, the Third Circuit clerk initialed and marked the Notice of Appeal with "RECEIVED" on March 3, 2026, and it was marked as "Electronically Filed" in this Intermediate Court of 
Appeals on March 4, 2026. 
(8) In the context of conventional submissions to a 
court, the controlling rule in Hawaiʻi has been that the date of 
4   FOR PUBLICATION IN WEST'S HAWAIʻI REPORTS AND PACIFIC REPORTER 
receipt prevails over the file-stamped date. This principle was 
firmly established in Doe v. Doe
, 98 Hawaiʻ i 144, 150-51, 44 P.3d 
1085, 1091-92 (2002), wherein the Hawai
ʻi Supreme Court held that 
the family court clerk's acceptance and date-stamping of a 
motion as "received" satisfied the jurisdictional requirement at issue in that case. The supreme court reaffirmed this holding 
in DL v. CL, 146 Hawaiʻ i 415, 421-22, 463 P.3d 1072, 1078-79 
(2020), wherein it concluded that this court had erred in 
concluding that certain post-divorce motions were untimely, because the motions were submitted to the family court clerk and stamped received on the deadline, but not stamped as filed until fifteen days later. See also Yuen v. Bd. of App. of Cnty. of
Haw., 2012 WL 902231, at *4-5 (Haw. App. Mar. 19, 2012) (Mem. 
Op.) (holding that a notice of appeal submitted to clerk and 
stamped received on the deadline date was sufficient for jurisdictional purposes notwithstanding that it was officially file-stamped days after the deadline). 
(9) In light of this well-established precedent, we 
hold that in the context of the Third Circuit (Hawaiʻ i island) 
Online Document Drop-off service, as currently established, a notice of appeal that is electronically stamped "Received electronically" by the Judiciary's online systems on the deadline date is sufficient for jurisdiction purposes, notwithstanding that it was not marked as received by the court clerk until a later date and then subsequently filed in this 
court. The use of an online process that is billed as a 
"convenient alternative to submit documents to the court" cannot be construed by this court as, in essence, a trap for the unwary self-represented person, even though the filing process is now three steps – electronically received, received by the clerk, and then file-stamped – rather than the two steps involved in the conventional filing cases. Here, the Notice of Appeal was
