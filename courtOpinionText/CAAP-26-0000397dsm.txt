(c)  "In general, appeals in family court cases, as in  
other civil cases, may be taken only from (1) a final judgment,  
order, or decree, . . . or (2) a certified interlocutory order."   
In re Doe, 96 Hawaiʻi 272, 283, 30 P.3d 878, 889 (2001) (citing

(d) A post-decree order is appealable if it finally 
determines all issue s raised in the post -decree proceeding. 
Hall v. Hall , 96 Hawaiʻi 105, 111 n.4, 26 P.3d 594, 600 n.4 (App. 
2001), affirmed in part, and vacated in part on other grounds , 
95 Hawaiʻi 318, 22 P.3d 695 (2001) . 
(e) This court's October 26, 2026 Order to Show Cause 
stated "[i]t appears that this appeal is premature and this 
court lacks appellate jurisdiction as the May 4, 2026 Order does 
not finally determine all issues raised in the post -decree 
proceeding, and the record in the underlying case indicates that 
the family court h as not yet concluded the evidentiary hearing. " 
The court instructed Mother to "file a statement to show cause 
why this appeal should not be dismissed for lack of appellate 
jurisdiction " within 14 days, and stated that "[f]ailure by 
Mother to respond to this order, or to show cause, may result in 
the dismissal of this appeal for lack of jurisdiction. " The 
court further instructed that "[a]ny other party may file a 
statement regarding appellate jurisdiction" within 14 days. 
(f) Mother did not file a response to the October 26, 
2026 Order to Show Cause , and no other party filed a statement. 
(g) When the court "perceive[s] a jurisdictional 
defect in an appeal, [it] must, sua sponte, dismiss that
