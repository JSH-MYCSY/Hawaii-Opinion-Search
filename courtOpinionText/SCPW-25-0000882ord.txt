ORDER DENYING PETITIONS AND MOTION FOR STAY    
(By: Devens, C.J., McKenna, Eddins, and Ginoza, JJ., 
and Circuit Judge Holma, assigned by reason of vacancy) 
Upon consideration of the petition for writ of mandamus 
and/or prohibition filed December 19, 2025, the motion for stay   
filed January 8, 2026, the first amended petition for  
extraordinary writ filed May 21, 2026, and the record,   
Petitioner has not demonstrated a clear and indisputable right 
to relief nor a lack of alternative means to obtain the relief 
sought, such as seeking relief through normal appellate 
procedures.   See Womble Bond Dickinson (US) LLP v. Kim       , 153 
Hawaiʻi 307, 319, 537 P.3d 1154, 1166 (2023); see also Eaton v.  
Eaton, 7 Haw. App. 111, 118–19, 748 P.2d 801, 805 (App. 1987).        
 
 
  
 
  
  
  
 
   
   
 
 
     
 
 
      
       It is ordered that the petitions are denied, and the motion 
for stay is denied as moot. 
It is further ordered:  
1. Docket 1 shall remain sealed because docket 1 contains 
unredacted personal information (names of minors) in violation 
of Rules 2.19 and 9 of the Hawaiʻ i Court Records Rules (HCRR). 
2. The clerk shall file a redacted docket 1. 
3. Docket 5 is stricken because the docket contains 
unredacted personal information in violation of HCRR Rules 2.19 
and 9. 
4. Docket 12 shall remain sealed because the docket 
contains a notice or order to withhold income, which is required 
to be sealed upon filing. See Hawaiʻ i Family Court Rules (HFCR) 
Rule 7.2(a)(2) (eff. 2022); see also Hawaiʻ i Rules of Appellate 
Procedure Rule 2.1(a) (adopting the HFCR, among other court 
rules, as part of these rules whenever applicable).
